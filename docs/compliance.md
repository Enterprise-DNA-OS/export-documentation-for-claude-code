# Rule checks for an exporter's documentation desk

Checked 30 October 2026. Run `npm run trade -- compliance` (or `/compliance`, `/origin-check`, `/entries`, `/lcs`). These checks find shipments, products, credits and settings whose records look inconsistent with the rules below. They check what is recorded, not the goods, the tariff classification or the agreement text itself, and they do not replace your customs broker, MPI, your bank or legal advice. The `jurisdiction` setting in `trade.settings` (NZ or AU) and the threshold, hours and years beside it hold the numbers each check uses; change them there, with the source.

| Check | What the data shows | Rule and source | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| export-entry | A booked shipment above `entry_threshold_cents` (FOB, base currency) has no export entry, and its lodgement time has passed or is inside 48 hours. A shipment recorded as on board with no entry is a breach. | New Zealand: goods must not be loaded for export until an entry has been made and passed (Customs and Excise Act 2018, s 89(5)). A full export entry is needed above NZ$1,000 FOB; Customs advises lodging at least 48 hours before loading by sea and 9 hours by air. [Lodge your export entry](https://customs.govt.nz/business/export/lodge-your-export-entry), [s 89](https://www.nzlii.org/nz/legis/consol_act/caea2018199/s89.html). Australia: goods over A$2,000, and prohibited or permit goods at any value, need an export declaration. [ABF export requirements](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/exporting/how-to-export/export-requirements) | The cargo cut-off stands in for loading time. Set `entry_threshold_cents` to 200000 and the base currency to AUD for Australia. |
| entry-late | The entry was lodged after the lodgement time. | Same Customs guidance: a late entry risks missing the voyage or flight. | A warning; the record stays so the pattern shows. |
| origin | A shipment claims an agreement and a line has no six-digit tariff code, no origin determination for that agreement on the ship date, evidence that lapsed before the ship date, or is recorded as not originating. | CPTPP: a certification of origin must carry the six-digit HS classification and the origin criterion for each good (Chapter 3, Annex 3-B), and the exporter who certifies must be able to support it. [NZ Customs CPTPP information guide](https://www.customs.govt.nz/media/yunbg2u2/information-guide-cpttp.pdf), [Auckland Chamber CPTPP origin FAQs](https://aucklandchamber.co.nz/international-trade/export-document-certification/cptpp-origin-faqs/) | It reads the tariff code and the determination you record. It does not classify goods or test a product-specific rule. |
| origin-validity | A certification of origin (self-certified or chamber) on a shipment was issued more than the agreement's `validity_days` before the ship date, or its blanket period ended before it. | CPTPP: a certification of origin is valid for one year after the date it is issued, unless the importing party allows longer (Article 3.20.5); a blanket certification covers a period of up to 12 months. Same sources. | Other agreements: set `validity_days` from the agreement text when you add it. |
| certifier-details | A self-certified claim is booked and the certifier's name, address, phone or email is missing from settings. | CPTPP Annex 3-B, paragraph 2: the certifier's name, address (including country), telephone number and email address. [Auckland Chamber CPTPP origin FAQs](https://aucklandchamber.co.nz/international-trade/export-document-certification/cptpp-origin-faqs/) | The printed certification shows REQUIRED in any empty field. |
| before-loading | A document marked `before_loading` (export health certificate, export permit) is missing or in draft within `cutoff_warn_days` of cut-off, or the goods shipped without it. | Food and animal products: the official assurance (export certificate) the importing country requires comes from MPI before export. [MPI exporting](https://www.mpi.govt.nz/export/). Strategic and dual-use goods: an MFAT export permit before export, with the entry number kept with your records. [MFAT export controls](https://www.mfat.govt.nz/en/trade/export-controls/how-do-i-use-my-export-permit/) | The operator sets which products need which. `/ship` refuses without them unless someone accepts it by name. |
| documents | Any other required document missing or in draft within `cutoff_warn_days` of cut-off (the bill of lading is not expected before loading), or one of our own documents missing after shipping. | Operator policy in this base: the full set is ready before cut-off. What a shipment needs comes from the market, the customer, the credit, the claim and the incoterm. | Change `required_docs` on the market or `extra_docs` on the customer. |
| documents-to-customer | Goods are on the water, arriving within `arrival_warn_days`, and documents the customer needs have not been sent. | Operator policy: the importer cannot clear the goods without the invoice, packing list, bill of lading and certificates. Not applied to letter of credit shipments, whose documents go through the bank. | |
| lc-shipment-date | A shipment under a letter of credit sails, or is booked to sail, after the credit's latest shipment date. | The credit's own terms (field 44C of an MT700). A late shipment is a discrepancy the bank can refuse. | `/book` refuses it unless overridden by name. |
| lc-presentation | Documents for a shipped credit shipment have not been presented and the last day is inside `lc_warn_days`, or has passed. | UCP 600, sub-article 14(c): a presentation including original transport documents must be made no later than 21 calendar days after the date of shipment, and in any event no later than the expiry date of the credit, unless the credit sets another period. [ICC discussion of 14(c)](https://library.iccwbo.org/content/tfb/dcinsight/DCI_Vol14n1_DiscussionPoint.htm) | `presentation_days` holds the credit's own period (21 when it is silent). |
| lc-amount | Orders under a credit come to more than its amount plus its tolerance. | The credit's amount and tolerance (fields 32B and 39A of an MT700). | |
| lc-expiry | A credit expires within twice `lc_warn_days` with goods still to ship. | The credit's expiry date. | A warning: ask for an extension in time. |
| records | A shipment that left more than 30 days ago has a required document never recorded. | New Zealand: customs-related business records are kept for seven years, in New Zealand unless Customs authorises otherwise. [NZ Customs business records guidance](https://customs.govt.nz/link/663c4301e49c40c0b27d7b0faa03c686.aspx). Australia: commercial documents relating to exported goods are kept for five years after export (Customs Act 1901, s 240). [Customs Act s 240 summary](https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/criminal/legislation/customs-act/failing-to-keep-commercial-documents) | `records_years` holds the period for your country. Nothing here deletes a record. |

To clear a finding, fix the record: lodge the entry and record its number, record the tariff code your broker confirms, record the origin evidence and its end date, issue a fresh certification of origin, add the certifier's details, apply for the health certificate or permit, chase the chamber or insurer, ask the buyer to amend the credit, present the documents, or send the set to the customer. Never edit the rule to make a finding disappear. If a rule here is out of date, update this file and the check in a new migration together, with the date and source.

Not checked here: the tariff classification itself, product-specific rules of origin and regional value content sums, the agreement texts other than CPTPP (add them with their own validity and statement), importing-country labelling, sanctions and restricted-party screening, dangerous goods declarations, phytosanitary certificates for plant products (add a document type when you need it), and whether the documents agree with each other word for word, which banks check under ISBP. Those need their own evidence.
